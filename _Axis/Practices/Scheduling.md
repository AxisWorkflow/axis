# Scheduling
> **Purpose:** Propose due bounded Jobs without treating a clock as authority.

Main owns definitions in `Schedules/{ID}.md`, immutable revision Messages and `SCHEDULES.md`. A definition names Project UUID, target, typed trigger/action, timezone, authority/expiry, output/notification, finite limits, overlap/missed/retry policies and explicit placement. New definitions are draft. Preview the exact local/UTC times, scope and maximum exposure before activation under User's mandate. Host trigger installation is a separate action.

Use `scheduling.py` for supported once, local daily/weekly calendar, fixed-rate/completion-relative interval and verified event triggers. Reject unsupported recurrence and missing timezone conversion explicitly; manual operation may use a resolved one-time instant. Default DST policy skips nonexistent times and selects the first repeated time; record skipped/coalesced work. Default missed policy coalesces one within expiry; default overlap queues at most one with no simultaneous occurrence. Hold uncertain clock changes. Bound catch-up count and age.

The occurrence key binds Project, Schedule, immutable execution revision and planned UTC instant or verified event ID. Under Main's valid lease, active Coordination placement and qualified storage, deduplicate before Job admission and reserve shared capacity. Metadata edits cannot replay admitted/completed one-shot work. Admitted Jobs retain their pinned revision; superseded pending candidates get an explicit mapping. Pause admits nothing new; resume applies bounded missed-work policy.

Admission is not execution: retain the admitted Job, persisted dispatch, actual worker result and Main validation as separate facts. A `prepare` Schedule admits a `report` Job; dispatch that permitted operation, with every report, checkpoint and readiness file inside its declared scope. Producing the scoped report does not grant general write authority. Re-evaluating a one-shot occurrence must not duplicate its Job or accepted output. Retained locks follow [Practices > Coordination > Retained locks and uncertain effects].

Dispatch only through [Practices > Coordination]. A timer cannot grant approval, change roles, publish, access Secrets or renew Main's heartbeat. Supervisory schedules initially permit only status/inspect; recurring child starts or mutation require a separate explicit standing mandate. A recurring definition expires by default after 24 hours of authority; no indefinite mandate is inferred.

Legacy schedules without `limits.budget` retry at most twice. Native schedules have no independent retry-count ceiling; only classified safe/idempotent failures receive retry offers, with bounded backoff inside current authority and the cumulative budget. A retry offer is not a dispatch or permission to replay an uncertain effect. Retain failures. A failed completion-relative Job does not become a new recurrence; a retained result awaits validation, and an unknown external effect awaits reconciliation.

An external timer may call the typed one-shot trigger interface, or request genuine Main startup. It never executes arbitrary schedule text. Without a configured trigger, show checkpoint-driven and next due/overdue work; do not promise wake-up. Missing Scheduling never blocks ordinary Axis. Persist logical intent in ENVIRONMENT with a Schedule reference; host handles, paths and credentials remain local. Copies require explicit placement activation. The local executor binding uses an existing host instance hint when available; otherwise bind and evaluate in one Main process. A different process reports the stable-binding limitation. Use the explicit `scheduling.py --root PROJECT_PATH --session SID manual-evaluate SCHEDULE_ID --executor LOGICAL_NAME --activate` path to bind, activate and evaluate one Schedule in one process; this can resume a paused Schedule and therefore requires current User authority. A timer cannot create the missing hint.

Old schedule Notes are migration inputs, not authority: preserve them, map reviewed definitions and start paused when intent/authority is uncertain. No service or recurring job is installed by migration. Record outcomes before notification; notification retries do not repeat the work.

If revision persistence succeeded but its mutable view update failed, inspect the retained linear revision chain and use `recover-revision SCHEDULE_ID REVISION_ID` for the exact accepted tip. Recovery validates source integrity, clears unadmitted candidates and leaves the Schedule paused. Forks, gaps and non-tip selections remain held; never delete history or replay an admitted occurrence to repair a view.

## Native budgets and scoped capacity

New definitions can supply the same `limits.budget` version 1 policy described in [Resources > Coordination-Manual > Native money/runtime budgets]. The scheduler carries that exact policy and original clock into every admitted occurrence; a new occurrence, definition revision or retry never resets its cumulative account. Native `duration_seconds`, launch/round/reserve estimates and `max_retries` do not create additional budget ceilings. The occurrence/missed-work queue and clock-integrity policies still apply in their own scheduling scope.

An exhausted native budget retains a paused Job. `due` reports it in `budget_holds`, with its ID, reasons and `extension_required`, rather than listing it as newly admitted execution. Inspect and settle reservations or accept the explicitly bound cumulative extension, then resume the retained Job under current authority. A completed/failed/expired Job is not reopened by re-evaluating its Schedule. Keep manual result capture, actual worker execution and accepted completion distinct.

`limits.concurrency` applies to the individual Job. Optional positive integer `limits.aggregate_concurrency` expresses an explicit shared project admission cap; it passes through to the Job unchanged. The project `Max Concurrent Sessions` policy and occupied or uncertain holders remain enforced. Two Jobs with local concurrency one can each occupy one slot when project and route capacity permit. A dormant unrelated local policy is not a global provider limit. No schedule definition installs a host timer or provides an observed route capacity.
