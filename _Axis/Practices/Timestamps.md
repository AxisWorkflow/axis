# Timestamps
> **Purpose:** Mint identifier timestamps that are unique across the entire Project, using an owned atomic claim, a post-claim existence check, and exclusive creation.

Every record in an Index-Detail directory is named by its timestamp, and that name is the record's identity (see [Practices > IndexDetail > The Name Is the Contract]). This Practice adds one guarantee on top: no identifier timestamp names two records anywhere in the Project. That uniqueness is what turns a bare timestamp into an unambiguous key - `grep -rn "{timestamp}"` from the project root finds THE record plus every cross-reference to it, with nothing to disambiguate. Clocks can repeat or move backward, and concurrent writers can choose the same millisecond. The existence check covers historical collisions as well as concurrency. Real milliseconds are what make that window a millisecond wide instead of a second wide - which is why step 1 goes to some trouble to get them, and why `000` is never an acceptable stand-in.

## Scope

- An identifier timestamp is one that becomes a filename in the twelve Index-Detail directories or their `_Axis/Archive/` families. Only identifiers pass through this procedure.
- Metadata stamps are values, not identities: Flag bodies and Line 2 refreshes, `reviewed:` and `completed:` field values, times quoted in prose. Mint them directly; never check or claim them.
- Initial Main and External Markers use the narrow Bootstrap Markers exception below. Their identifiers still require a claim, a post-claim check, and exclusive creation; only the prior-Marker lease check is exempt.
- A rename keeps identity: archiving a record, or repairing a malformed name under `^refresh`, moves an existing identity - it is not a new mint (the existence check still guards the repaired name's destination).
- Each Subproject is its own uniqueness domain. Never scan into a nested Subproject and never claim across the parent/child boundary - separate processes may be running against each side.

## Minting an Identifier

1. Generate a candidate. Run this one command exactly and use what it prints:

		TS=$(date -u +"%Y.%m.%d.%H.%M.%S.%3NZ"); case "$TS" in *3N*) TS=$(node -e 'console.log(new Date().toISOString().replace(/[-T:]/g,"."))' 2>/dev/null || python3 -c 'import datetime as d;n=d.datetime.now(d.timezone.utc);print(n.strftime("%Y.%m.%d.%H.%M.%S.")+"%03dZ"%(n.microsecond//1000))' 2>/dev/null || perl -MTime::HiRes=time -e 'my $t=time;my @g=gmtime $t;printf "%04d.%02d.%02d.%02d.%02d.%02d.%03dZ",$g[5]+1900,$g[4]+1,$g[3],$g[2],$g[1],$g[0],($t-int $t)*1000');; esac; echo "$TS"

	BSD/macOS `date` has no `%N` and prints a literal `3N` where the milliseconds belong; the `case` catches exactly that and falls back to an interpreter that has them. Never substitute `000` for the milliseconds. Zeros are not a lower-precision timestamp, they are a fabricated field in an identifier ([Rules > Timestamps]), and they collapse every mint in the same second onto one value - measured 2026-08-06, all 30 records written that day on a macOS host ended `.000Z`, which is the entire collision risk this Practice exists to remove. If a shell runs but no interpreter answers, use `000`, treat steps 2 and 3 as load-bearing rather than routine, and Log it ONCE per session as a Capability downgrade (`Capability downgrade: timestamp precision`, per [Practices > Logs > Capability Downgrades]) with `behavior-used: zeroed milliseconds, uniqueness carried by the existence check and claim`. Never narrate it per mint - that is exactly the noise [Rules > Speaking] exists to prevent, and uniqueness holds without User's help. But DO queue ONE boot-time line, alongside the other host-capability notices Session Start delivers: a host that cannot produce milliseconds at all is something User can act on (install `node`, `python3`, or `perl`), and "would User's next action change?" is the test that decides. Measured T1, 2026-08-06: an earlier blanket ban on speaking failed its own test - the agent told User anyway, and it was RIGHT to, because the news was a host gap plus a defect in this very ladder. The Log is the durable record and `^audit` is the reader - it scans on-disk identifiers for the `.000Z` signature - so the fallback stays visible where visibility is useful, spoken once where it is actionable, and silent everywhere else.

2. Prepare the record body before acquiring anything. An optional early existence check may skip occupied candidates, but it is never the authoritative check. The complete uniqueness domain is:

		find _Axis/{Agents,Audit,CX,Followups,Ideas,Logs,Notes,Reminders,Snapshots,Status,Supervision,Tasks} _Axis/Archive -name "{candidate}.md" -print

	Distinguish an empty successful listing from inaccessible directories or failed tools. Without a shell, use equivalent host file tools. Increment milliseconds with normal second/day carry when a candidate is occupied.

3. Verify your lease, THEN claim the value. READ your own Marker `_Axis/Agents/{Session ID}.md` per [Practices > Markers > The Lease] and check its tombstone immediately before the claim. A tombstone stops you; a bare-missing Marker is stop-and-ask. Only Bootstrap Markers below bypass this prior-Marker check. Take the Timestamp Claim unconditionally for every identifier: atomically `mkdir "_Temp/{candidate}.tsclaim"`, then create and read back its `OWNER` file containing a fresh random nonce and your Session ID (or the bootstrap candidate). Retain that exact owner token. An existing directory means contention: increment the candidate and retry. Permission, missing-parent, storage, and other I/O failures are errors, not collisions: stop the affected write instead of looping. With no exclusive primitive, use only the confirmed single-writer maintenance window described in [Claim-Session]; do not pretend a check-then-write is atomic. In that fallback no filesystem claim exists: revalidate the still-exclusive window in place of `OWNER`, and skip claim-release operations. Keep the window through record readback (through startup completion for a bootstrap).

4. AFTER acquiring the claim, recheck the entire uniqueness domain from step 2. If any record already uses the value, release only your own claim and retry with the next candidate. This recheck is mandatory even when an early scan was empty: another writer may have committed and released in between. Revalidate the owner token, lease (except bootstrap), and tombstone; create the record with exclusive create/no-overwrite semantics. A destination that appeared concurrently is never overwritten. In the confirmed sole-writer window only, if exclusive create is unavailable, check exact absence immediately before normal creation and require readback; this claims no concurrent safety. Read back the exact record before creating its index entry. Keep this prepared critical section under 10 seconds; if it expires, stop and leave recovery to the quiescent protocol, never continue a delayed write. Do not hold a claim while researching, reasoning, or waiting for User.

5. Release only the claim whose `OWNER` still matches your retained token: remove that file and `rmdir` the claim directory. On mismatch or failed removal, stop and report; never remove a replacement owner's claim. The committed record now reserves the identity permanently. Delete-blocked claims remain for recovery; never clear them in place or use recursive deletion at an active claim name. STOP.

## Bootstrap Markers

A fresh Main or External has no prior Marker to read. Only the first Marker of a newly booting session may bypass the prior-Marker lease check in step 3. Main must hold [Claim-Session] admission; External must have been routed by the entry protocol. Use the initial timestamp as a candidate, complete the claim and post-claim checks above, and commit the final Session ID only after they succeed. Hold the claim through exclusive Marker creation and exact readback, then release it. Check for a `.kill` sibling before and after creation. If a tombstone exists, stop; it is not permission to mint another identity. A partial/failed bootstrap stops without a success greeting.

This exception never renews, repairs, or recreates a lost existing lease, never applies to a Subagent with an assigned Marker, and never permits a boot Log or ordinary record before the first Marker validates. Subsequent writes use the normal lease check. A timestamp increment before identity commitment is collision handling; minting a new identity after lease loss is forbidden.

## Why the Claim Is Unconditional

Claim every identifier, whether or not another writer has been noticed. The exclusive claim plus the post-claim full-domain check closes the check/create gap, including the schedule in which another writer completes and releases its claim between your early scan and acquisition. Exclusive file creation supplies a final no-overwrite guard. An existing timestamp is advanced deterministically by one millisecond; never randomize the timestamp field.

## Stale Claims

An expired claim is evidence of an interrupted or delayed writer, not proof that the writer cannot resume. Session Start and `^refresh` inventory and report stale `*.tsclaim/` directories; they never rename or delete them while writers may be active. Reclaim only under [Lock-File > Quiescent Recovery], with all other writers and pending launches stopped for the entire maintenance window. Ordinary minting skips an occupied candidate without stealing it.

## Duplicates Found Later

Records created before this Practice, or slipped past it, can still collide. `^audit` scans for duplicate identifier names read-only; `^refresh` repairs them with User approval: keep the older or index-linked record under the contested name, rename the other by incrementing its milliseconds to the first free value, and relink its mutable index entry when it has one. Renaming repairs identity, not content - it does not violate WORM - and a stale reference inside an old WORM record is history, not a defect.
